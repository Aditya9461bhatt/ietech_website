## What i.e tech does

i.e tech offers **i.e ERP** for manufacturing — a single platform that runs the entire production
operation, from planning and the bill of materials through shop-floor execution, quality control, and
costing. It replaces the mix of human knowledge, spreadsheets, and disconnected accounting software
that most manufacturers rely on, and "forces discipline and automation into the pipeline."

The goal is to bring the whole manufacturing business onto one powerful platform so teams stop
switching between disconnected tools — eliminating bottlenecks and optimizing throughput.

## Who it's for

- Modern manufacturers replacing fragmented legacy systems
- Shops running complex, multi-operation production processes
- Manufacturers whose planning, tracking, and costing currently live in spreadsheets and separate
  accounting software

## How it operates

The modules are connected, so planning, execution, and costing stay in sync:

1. **Plan** — production plans and the Master Production Schedule set what to build; MRP checks
   material needs and auto-generates purchase/material requests to prevent stockouts.
2. **Define the recipe** — a multi-level BOM specifies raw materials, sub-assemblies, consumables,
   and scrap, with accurate costing built in.
3. **Route the work** — operations are defined and mapped to workstations, with enforced sequences
   and operating costs based on hourly rates.
4. **Execute on the floor** — work orders move operation by operation (milling → turning → assembly →
   quality), tracked live with produced quantities and material transfer status.
5. **Track jobs and time** — job cards capture actual operator time, compare planned vs. actual cost,
   and log downtime with pause/resume.
6. **Enforce quality** — inspections at receipt and during production apply custom criteria and sample
   sizes, and automatically quarantine rejected items.
7. **Cost precisely** — final cost = raw materials + operations − scrap, giving exact pricing instead
   of estimates.
8. **Plan capacity** — bottlenecks at overloaded workstations are identified and scheduling respects
   working hours and holidays, with shop-floor load visualized in real time.
