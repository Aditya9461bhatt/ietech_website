# The Problem

A busy forging plant supplying machined components to OEM customers was running production the way most of the industry does: order book on paper, planning by phone call, and stock counted by walking the yard. It worked — until volumes grew.

Quotes took days because costing lived in one senior manager's head. Dispatches were delayed not by capacity but by coordination: material would be ready while the paperwork chased signatures. And with no live view of work-in-progress, promising delivery dates to customers was guesswork.

# The Approach

We spent the first two weeks on the floor before configuring anything — mapping how an enquiry became a dispatch, where information changed hands, and where it got lost. That map, not a feature list, decided the deployment.

The rollout then went stage by stage: sales and order management first, then stores and purchase, then production planning against actual machine capacity, and finally dispatch and invoicing — each stage run in parallel with the old process until the team trusted it.

# The Solution

- **One order chain.** Enquiry, quotation, order confirmation, production plan, and dispatch now live in a single flow — nothing re-typed, nothing lost between departments.
- **Live work-in-progress.** Every heat and machining operation is booked against its work order, so "where is my order?" is a lookup, not an investigation.
- **Stores with numbers, not folklore.** Material receipts, issues, and yard stock are recorded at the point of movement; purchasing runs on actual consumption.
- **Costing on real data.** Job-wise material and operation costs accumulate automatically, so quoting new parts takes [hours, not days].
